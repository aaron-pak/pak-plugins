How an order gets from a phone to the counter

Six parts, and only one of them is ours to keep running. Read this before your first change; it takes about five minutes.

The map

Everything goes through the orders database

In clay: the orders database, the only part on our own server. The other five are a phone, a tablet and three services we pay for.

Follow one order

An oat latte, start to finish

Tuesday 10 November, 8:02 am, a busy morning. Under a minute from tapping Pay to the order on the screen, most of it waiting for the bank.

  1. 8:02:10

    Pay is tapped

    The order site asks the payment company to charge £3.40.

  2. 8:02:14

    The bank says yes

    Four seconds is normal. Over ten, the site says so and keeps waiting.

  3. 8:02:15

    The order is saved

    Only paid orders reach the database, so the kitchen never makes an unpaid drink.

  4. 8:02:16

    It’s on the kitchen screen

    Fourth in the queue, behind three orders from the counter.

  5. 8:05:40

    Marked made, text sent

    The barista taps it done and the customer gets “Your oat latte is ready”.

Before your first change

Two rules

Never test with a real card

The payment company has a test mode with pretend cards. Your machine uses it unless you change PAYMENTS_MODE.

Ship after 2 pm

Mornings are the rush. A change that goes out after lunch has hours to settle before the next one.
